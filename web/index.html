Golden rules

  1. Decide your stop before you buy, and never move it lower.
  2. Risk the same small amount on every trade (about 1% of your account).
  3. Stop for the day after 3 losses in a row. Never try to "win it back".
  4. Don't chase. If you missed the entry, wait for the next setup.
  5. Best hours: the first 90 minutes after the open.
Learn: how it works and what everything means

How the app picks stocks

  1. Finds the crowd: scans the whole market for stocks up 8% or more, priced $1.50 to $25.
  2. Checks Ross's 5 pillars: price, % gain, relative volume, fresh news, small float.
  3. Researches each one across 4 sources: Alpaca (prices and news), Yahoo Finance (float, short interest, ownership, company), Finviz (backup float and more headlines) and SEC EDGAR (official filings that reveal share offerings).
  4. Scores it out of 100 on 11 rules and finds the entry: a short, quiet pullback after a run, then a break above the pullback's high.
  5. Maps the key levels (high of day, pre-market high, yesterday's high, 52-week high, round numbers) to see where sellers will show up.
  6. Filters out traps: dilution news, offering prospectuses at the SEC, wide spreads, thin stocks, prices under VWAP, and stocks that already ran.

What each signal means

BUY NOWIt just broke out, the score is 85% or higher, and nothing blocks it.
BUY SMALLIt broke out, but some rules are missing (70 to 84%). Use half size.
GET READYA pullback has formed just under the trigger. Set an alert at the entry.
WATCHIt's moving but has no safe entry yet. The app tells you what it's waiting for.
TOO LATEIt ran past the entry. Chasing gives a bad reward for the risk.
SKIPA trap: dilution, a wide spread, too few trades, or a low score.

Float and ownership, explained

Float
Shares that can actually be traded (total shares minus those locked up by insiders). Under 10M is low: a small supply, so the price moves very fast.
Float rotation
Today's full-market volume ÷ the float. 1x means every tradable share changed hands today. Ross's biggest winners often rotate the float several times.
Short % of float
Shares borrowed and sold by traders betting the price will fall. Above 15%, a strong rise can force them to buy back (a short squeeze).
Days to cover
How many days of normal volume shorts would need to buy back. Higher means more squeeze fuel.
Institutions / insiders
Big funds holding a lot makes a stock calmer. Low institutional ownership means retail traders drive it, so it's wilder.

SEC filings, explained

424B (prospectus)
Shares are being sold now. This is the clearest dilution signal, so the app blocks the trade for 10 days.
S-1 / S-3 / F-1 / F-3
The company registered shares it can sell later (a "shelf"). Rallies often get sold into.
EFFECT
The SEC approved a registration: the company can now sell shares at any time.
8-K / 6-K
A material event (deal, results, management change). It often explains the move.
NT 10-Q / NT 10-K
The company is late with its financial report: a warning sign.
Form 4
An insider bought or sold shares.
