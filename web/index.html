Golden rules

  1. Decide your stop before you buy, and never move it lower.
  2. Only buy on BUY NOW. GET READY is not a buy.
  3. Stop for the day after 3 losses in a row. Never try to "win it back".
  4. Don't chase. If you missed the entry, wait for the next setup.
  5. Best hours: the first 90 minutes after the open.
Learn: how it works and what everything means

How the app picks stocks

  1. Finds the crowd: scans the whole market for stocks up 8% or more, priced $1.50 to $25.
  2. Checks Ross's 5 pillars: price, % gain, relative volume, fresh news, small float.
  3. Researches each one across 4 sources: Alpaca (prices and news), Yahoo Finance (float, short interest, ownership, company), Finviz (backup float and more headlines) and SEC EDGAR (official filings that reveal share offerings).
  4. Runs a 20-point checklist (9 MUST items, the rest weighted) and finds the entry: a short, quiet pullback after a run, then a break above its high on a volume surge.
  5. Follows every BUY to the exit: the plan is frozen, then HOLD, SELL HALF at +1R, EXIT at the stop or target. Results go on the scoreboard.
  6. Maps the key levels (high of day, pre-market high, yesterday's high, 52-week high, round numbers) to see where sellers will show up.
  7. Filters out traps: dilution news, offering prospectuses at the SEC, wide spreads, thin stocks, prices under VWAP, and stocks that already ran.

What each signal means

BUY NOWIt broke out the right way, passed every MUST check, and is missing at most 2 points of the weighted checks. The only time to buy.
HOLDYou're in. The plan is frozen: same stop, same target. Do nothing.
SELL HALFIt reached +1R. Sell half and move your stop to your entry price: the trade can't lose anymore.
EXIT NOWStop hit, lost VWAP, or no follow-through in 10 minutes. Sell everything. Small loss, as planned.
GET READYHeads-up only. Don't buy. If it breaks out the right way, you get BUY NOW.
WAITNo safe entry yet. The row tells you what it's waiting for.
TOO LATEIt ran past the entry. Don't chase.
NO TRADEA MUST check failed (dilution, below VWAP, weak volume, no room, daily loss limit...). Don't buy.

Float and ownership, explained

Float
Shares that can actually be traded (total shares minus those locked up by insiders). Under 10M is low: a small supply, so the price moves very fast.
Float rotation
Today's full-market volume ÷ the float. 1x means every tradable share changed hands today. Ross's biggest winners often rotate the float several times.
Short % of float
Shares borrowed and sold by traders betting the price will fall. Above 15%, a strong rise can force them to buy back (a short squeeze).
Days to cover
How many days of normal volume shorts would need to buy back. Higher means more squeeze fuel.
Institutions / insiders
Big funds holding a lot makes a stock calmer. Low institutional ownership means retail traders drive it, so it's wilder.

Level 2, explained

Bid / Ask
Bid = the best price a buyer is offering right now. Ask = the cheapest price a seller accepts. Level 1 shows only those two; Level 2 shows every price level behind them.
Size and "who"
Shares waiting at each price, and who posted them: exchanges (NSDQ, ARCA, EDGX, BATS, MEMX) or market makers (NITE, VIRT, CDRG, GSCO...). Market makers trade both ways, so one name alone means little.
Wall
A level 3x+ bigger than normal. A seller wall caps the price until it's eaten; a buyer wall acts as support until it's hit through.
Eaten / hit through
The wall traded away. A seller wall eaten = real breakout. A buyer wall hit through = support broke.
Pulled (spoof)
A wall vanished without trading. It was likely there to scare or lure traders. Never trust a level that keeps appearing and disappearing.
Hidden seller / buyer (iceberg)
Far more shares trade at one price than were ever shown, and the order keeps refilling. A big player is hiding size: a hidden seller caps breakouts, a hidden buyer is strong support.
Imbalance
Shares bid vs offered near the price. 1.5x+ more bids = buyers stacked; 1.5x+ more asks = sellers stacked.
Thin / gap
Few orders between two prices. Price can jump through the gap fast, up or down.

SEC filings, explained

424B (prospectus)
Shares are being sold now. This is the clearest dilution signal, so the app blocks the trade for 10 days.
S-1 / S-3 / F-1 / F-3
The company registered shares it can sell later (a "shelf"). Rallies often get sold into.
EFFECT
The SEC approved a registration: the company can now sell shares at any time.
8-K / 6-K
A material event (deal, results, management change). It often explains the move.
NT 10-Q / NT 10-K
The company is late with its financial report: a warning sign.
Form 4
An insider bought or sold shares.
